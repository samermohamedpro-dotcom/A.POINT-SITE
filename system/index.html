From her phone to your hands

What happens when someone orders

  1. 01

    She chooses

    On your site. She sees only the days and times you can actually make for — full days are already closed.

  2. 02

    She presses Order

    The order opens in her WhatsApp, already written: products, quantities, delivery zone, total.

  3. 03

    It reaches you twice

    As a WhatsApp message, and as a card in your schedule. If the second ever fails, the first still arrives.

  4. 04

    You accept

    One tap. The customer record, the schedule entry and the sale are created together. Stock comes off.

  5. 05

    You make it, and get paid

    The way you already do. No card gateway, no commission, no platform between you and her.

WhatsApp is what makes the sale, always. The rest saves you the typing — and if any of it ever breaks, the order still reaches you.

What lands in your WhatsApp

Add something, and the message starts writing itself.

The example sells cakes; yours sells whatever you make. Your products, prices, currency, delivery zones and opening hours are set once, for you, at setup.

Two pieces, and no server

A public site your customers use, and a private app only you can open. Between them, a database. There is no server in the middle — nothing to administer, nothing to restart, nothing that falls over at three in the morning. That is deliberate, and it is why the running cost stays low enough for a one-person business.

The site can never create anything in your books. An order from the site arrives as a proposal, and it only becomes real when you accept it. That is a rule of the system, not a setting.

Six screens, and nothing else to learn

The private app

Built for someone who has never used business software, on a phone, with their hands full. Six tabs along the bottom. That is the whole thing.

01

Schedule

Today at the top: what is being delivered, and when to start making. Orders from the site arrive here as cards you accept or dismiss in one tap. You can close an hour or a whole day on the site from here.

02

Summary

Profit this month, and cash on hand today. Then what needs you, and what is running low with what to buy first. When you are busy it shows two numbers; when you are not, everything is one tap underneath.

03

Stock

Your ingredients in four families, folded away until you need them. Search, sort, and a clear count of what to buy. Stock comes off by itself as orders are accepted — you never type a subtraction.

04

Products

Your products, with the recipe behind each one — what goes into it. That recipe is what makes stock work, what deduces allergens for the site, and what tells you how many batches you can still make.

05

Sales

Every sale, with the price frozen as it was on the day. A price you change today never rewrites what you earned last month.

06

Purchases

What you bought, at what price. This is what turns "money came in" into "this is what I actually earned".

The part nobody else gives you

Numbers that are true, not encouraging

Most tools tell you what came in. That is revenue, and it feels wonderful. This tells you what is left after the materials, the boxes, the power and the driver — and how much of it is money you actually have.

Profit, not revenue

Every sale carries its real cost, taken from the recipe and the price you actually paid for the ingredients.

Cash on hand

What is in the bank, separate from the value of stock you have bought but not yet sold.

Prices frozen at the sale

Raise a price today and last month stays exactly as it was.

Where it comes from

How many visits turned into orders, and which link brought them — without a single third-party tracker.

Tested, not hoped

Why a wrong number never reaches you

More than nineteen hundred automated checks run before anything is released. They do not check that the code runs — they check that the answers are right.

Every calculation is checked

Profit, stock, delivery fees, production limits, the order message itself.

A check that has never failed proves nothing

Every guarantee here was verified by deliberately breaking the code it protects, and watching the check catch it.

An awkward check is never deleted

If a change breaks one, either the change is wrong, or the rule changed — and then the check is rewritten to state the new rule, with the reason.

Backed up every day

The whole database, automatically. And the restore has been run, not assumed.
